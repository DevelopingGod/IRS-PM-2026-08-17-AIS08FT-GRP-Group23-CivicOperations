1Executive summary

Did the historical dataset provide evidence that agency assignment requires more than an RT-code catalog lookup?
For choosing the agency and the crew skill: no. The historical first agency matched the catalog agency in 49,556 of 49,558 records (99.996%). The only 2 disagreements are 311 operator-desk rows (NYC311-PRD) from training. A learned majority classifier per code (C1) and per code and borough (C2) changed 0 assignments. No runtime-available input carries routing signal beyond the code itself.

For anticipating hand-offs: yes, for a small, specific set of codes. Of all 1,164 REFERRED requests, 503 fall in RT002, RT003, RT043. Their referrals follow patterns the agency matrix explains (plates on vehicles, sewer-caused cave-ins) but which CO1 does not capture. The component therefore adds an explainable, train-derived referral advisory (rule R14). It never overrides the shared catalog. On the untouched test split it flags 3.7% of requests and catches 46.6% of the requests that were later referred, 12.6× the base rate.

Selected method: Approach B, catalog plus knowledge-based business rules (R01–R13). It adds an informational historical referral advisory (R14) that was fitted on the training split, selected on validation and evaluated once on test. The runtime package depends only on the Python standard library and the shared catalog. It does not need the historical dataset.

Why not plain lookup (Approach A)? It assigns the same agencies, but on the 57 labelled CO1 scenarios it made 11 unsafe dispatches (911 cases, not-ready and human-review requests, malformed input). It also gives no explanation. Approach B made 0.

3Component scope

In scope

  • Validate the CO1 message (types, ranges, codes).
  • Apply the emergency, readiness and human-review gates.
  • Handle the special labels EMERGENCY, OTHER and SMALLTALK.
  • Look up the agency and crew skill(s) in the shared catalog.
  • Detect unknown codes and catalog conflicts, and fail safely.
  • Decide dispatch eligibility and the next workflow step.
  • Explain the decision; attach historical referral context (detail block).
  • Pass every agreed CO1 field on to duplicate detection unchanged in name.

Out of scope (other owners)

  • Classifying citizen text (intake bot) and deciding emergencies (emergency check).
  • Estimated service or resolution time (est_service_minutes is never read).
  • Urgency, prioritisation and external factors.
  • Duplicate, credibility and fake-request detection.
  • Geocoding, crew assignment, travel time and routes.

10Referral analysis

What does agency mean for a referred request? The closure note of every referred request was written by the agency in the agency column (100.0% of 1,164 notes where the author could be identified). That agency equals the catalog agency in 100.0% of cases. agency is therefore the initial agency. No structured column records the destination. All 1,164 referred rows have verified_outcome = INCONCLUSIVE.

Can the destination be recovered without speculation? Only partly. Parsing the templated closure notes (complete list below) gives an explicit destination for 560 requests (48.1%). Of the rest, 63 are hand-offs to an internal unit of the same agency (DOT inspections, arterial and bridge divisions), so the agency did not change. The others say only “appropriate agency”, “another agency” or “out of jurisdiction”. Referred records are therefore retrospective error indicators. They are not reliable training targets for a corrected agency, and no correction model was trained on invented targets.

11Temporal split and leakage controls

  • The supplied split column was used as is. There was no random resampling. Train ends 2026-05-20 06:43:24, validation runs to 2026-07-17 19:53:36, and test follows.
  • Train was used to fit every data-derived quantity: majority tables, referral rates, borough cells and the knowledge artifact (fitted_on_split: "train").
  • Validation was used to choose the estimator (RT vs RT+borough), both pseudo-counts and the advisory threshold. The selection rule was fixed before the test split was read.
  • Test was read once, for final reporting. Nothing was re-fitted afterwards.
  • Runtime inputs are only CO1 fields. The router never reads closed_date, resolution_description, outcome_class, verified_outcome or resolution_hours. outcome_class and closure notes from the training period serve as labels for static knowledge. Nothing from the current request's outcome is ever used, so the knowledge cannot leak future information about it.
  • Historical catalog_agency, required_skill and est_service_minutes are copies of the catalog (verified: identical). They were never used as evidence for the catalog, which would be circular.

12Knowledge-discovery findings

  1. The RT code determines the first agency. 99.996% agreement, 100% on validation and on test, and stable in every month (table view below).
  2. The historical label derives from the catalog taxonomy (99.3% exact reconstruction), so agreement is a consistency check.
  3. Referrals concentrate in a few codes. RT043, RT002, RT124 and RT003 hold 52.2% of all referrals.
  4. Referral rates per code are temporally stable. Pearson correlation of per-code rates: train vs validation 0.9706, train vs test 0.9568 (Spearman 0.7609).
  5. The matrix explains the two largest patterns. RT043 → NYPD when plates are attached (matrix: “DSNY or NYPD depending on plates”), and RT002 → DEP after DOT inspection (sewer and water causes are DEP). Neither deciding fact is known at intake.
  6. Unresolved patterns: 23 codes have referrals with no or partial destination evidence (for example RT124: NYPD notes never name the destination). These are listed in section 14.
  7. Data quality: location_type is missing in 6,623 rows, and 56 descriptor values occur fewer than 5 times. Neither affects the runtime, which uses neither field.

A, C1 and C2 are identical. Learning from history, with or without the borough, reproduces the catalog exactly. C3 is the strongest correction model the data allows. It changes one code, RT002 (DOT → DEP), and raises agreement with the determinable final agency on test from 98.56% to 98.91%. It was rejected for four reasons:

  1. It overrides the shared catalog, which shared rule 3 makes the key, without an agreed correction policy.
  2. DOT is not wrong as a first responder. The notes read “DOT inspected and has requested DEP address the issue”, and DOT re-inspects after 60 days. DEP's involvement follows DOT's inspection.
  3. No runtime input separates the RT002 cases that move to DEP from those that do not. Switching would misroute the remaining 19 test cases where DOT finished the job.
  4. The label is retrospective and depends on parsing free text.

The finding is raised for the group instead (section 22).

Referral-risk knowledge (data mining for the advisory)

Two smoothed estimators (m-estimates shrunk toward a prior) were tuned in two stages on validation. The pre-declared rule keeps RT-only unless RT+borough improves validation log-loss by more than 1% without lowering AUC. The relative gain was 1.67%, so RT+borough was selected (mRT = 5, mcell = 100). On test: AUC 0.8936 vs 0.8829 for RT-only, and log-loss 0.08623 vs 0.0872 (constant baseline 0.12674).

15Selected solution and justification

Approach B (catalog plus knowledge-based rules) with the R14 historical referral advisory, informational by default.
  • The catalog is sufficient for agency and skill selection. Every code is unique and complete, history agrees, and no learned alternative changed a single assignment from runtime inputs.
  • The rules are necessary for safety. Lookup alone made 11 unsafe dispatches out of 57 scenarios; the rules made 0. Every decision is explained and traceable to rule IDs.
  • History adds value only as context. The advisory concentrates attention: on test it covers 3.7% of requests and contains 46.6% of later referrals. It leaves the agreed catalog intact, and holding is opt-in (review_on_elevated_referral_risk) because shared rule 7 says nobody is refused automatically. Holding would delay the 65.5% of flagged requests that the first agency did handle.
  • It is portable. It needs only the standard library, a CSV and an optional 26 KB JSON, with no model files and no dataset at runtime.

ELEVATED at code level: RT002, RT003, RT043. Further code and borough cells that are ELEVATED only when the borough is known: RT124/BRONX.

R01Validate CO1 inputRequired fields, types, confidence range and request-type format must match the CO1 contract; otherwise the result is INVALID.
R02Emergency restrictionOnly emergency_action CONTINUE may proceed. STOP_CALL_911 blocks; CONFIRM_WITH_CITIZEN holds; contradictory emergency fields hold for a person.

R05Special request typesEMERGENCY blocks (citizen told to call 911); OTHER and SMALLTALK are held and follow the intake workflow instead of agency/skill dispatch.
R06Exact catalog lookupA well-formed RT code is resolved only by exact match in the shared catalog.
R07Unknown codesA code absent from the catalog is UNRESOLVED; no agency is invented.
R08Catalog integrityDuplicate-conflicting or incomplete catalog entries are never resolved by picking a row; the code is UNRESOLVED and flagged for catalog review.
R09CO1 confidenceIntake confidence is passed through unchanged as confidence and is not the probability that the agency is correct. An optional, configurable min_confidence holds low-confidence requests.
R10Required skillsSkills come only from the catalog entry; a missing skill makes the assignment UNRESOLVED.
R11ExplanationEvery result carries a human-readable explanation naming each blocking rule; the full list of rules applied is in the optional detail block.
R12Safe unresolved stateIf agency or skills cannot be justified, agency and skills are emptied and the result is held for review rather than guessed.
R13Downstream eligibilitydispatch_eligible requires a resolved agency and skills, ready_to_submit, no human-review need, no emergency block and a dispatchable catalog entry. It does not guarantee a crew can be sent.
R14Historical referral advisoryWhen train-period history shows the code's first agency often referred requests onward, attach an informational advisory. It never changes agency or skills; it holds the request only if review_on_elevated_referral_risk is configured.

Status precedence: INVALID > BLOCKED > HELD > UNRESOLVED > ASSIGNED. All rules run for every request, so the result lists every reason, not only the first. A rule can add a reason but can never remove one, which is why no combination of inputs can make a blocked request eligible. This is tested exhaustively over 96 gate combinations.

20Downstream integration

The next component in the shared pipeline is duplicate detection, then external factors, credibility, fake-request detection, resolution time, urgency, prioritisation and finally crew deployment. Requests with status = ASSIGNED carry next_step = DUPLICATE_DETECTION; every other request names where it goes instead. Duplicate detection reads request_type and the slots exactly as CO1 sent them. Crew deployment, at the end of the pipeline, uses the fields this component added:

if result["dispatch_eligible"]:
    candidates = [c for c in crews
                  if c["agency"] == result["agency"]
                  and set(result["required_skills"]) <= set(c["skills"].split("|"))]
    # then: prioritisation order, geocode result["slots"], borough match, capacity (max_jobs), routing
else:
    route_by(result["next_step"])   # HUMAN_REVIEW, CONFIRM_WITH_CITIZEN, ...
  • Agencies and skills are always from the shared vocabulary that the crew table uses (8 agencies, 16 skills). A mock downstream consumer in tests/test_integration.py verifies this through a JSON round trip.
  • dispatch_eligible = true is necessary, not sufficient. Priority, location quality (the address is text, and geocoding is unassigned in the shared document), crew availability and capacity are checked later.
  • Service time comes from the catalog's est_service_minutes, which the dispatch component should read itself with the same RT code. This component does not pass it on, by design.
  • Requests closed as REFERRED by crews should ideally record a structured destination agency. That would make a supervised correction model possible in future (section 22).

21Limitations

  • Perfect-intake assumption. The historical replay feeds the true RT code. Real CO1 misclassification is not measured here; the R09 threshold exists for that, but it is off by default.
  • Circular agreement. Historical RT codes derive from 311 complaint types through the catalog, so 100% agreement shows consistency, not accuracy.
  • Partial referral destinations. 51.9% of referrals name no destination, and REFERRED also includes internal-unit transfers.
  • Selection. The dataset contains only the 34 catalog complaint types. Requests that CO1 would label OTHER are absent.
  • Seasonality. RT044 (snow and ice) is absent from validation and test, so its advisory level is untested out of time.
  • Advisory precision. On test, 65.5% of flagged requests were not referred. The advisory is context, not a prediction about the individual request.
  • Analyst annotation. The code-to-matrix-family mapping is hand-made and documented, not part of the supplied references.
  • Platform coverage. Tested on Python 3.14.7 on Windows only; the declared minimum is 3.9.
  • Open contract points. Shared rule 2 says EMERGENCY labels are “held”, while the label table says “call 911, nothing is filed”. This component blocks them (DECISIONS.md, D-08).

22Conclusions and future recommendations

The shared catalog is a complete and historically consistent operational reference for agency and skill assignment. The component's intelligence lies in its explicit, explainable safety and integrity rules, and in knowledge discovered from referrals. No learned classifier was trained, because the evidence does not justify one: the catalog already explains the target, and no reliable corrected-agency label exists.

Recommendations for the group

  1. Vehicles (RT043/RT124): add a CO1 slot such as has_license_plate. The matrix says plates decide DSNY versus NYPD, and 40.2% of RT043 requests were referred, mostly to NYPD.
  2. Cave-ins (RT002) and defective street hardware (RT003): discuss DEP co-notification or a catalog sub-code. Most of these referrals go to DEP after DOT inspection.
  3. Structured referral outcome: when crew deployment closes a job as REFERRED, record the destination agency. That turns referrals into a usable supervised target.
  4. Public housing: NYCHA is in the matrix but not in the catalog. Decide how RT080–RT084 in NYCHA buildings should be handled.
  5. Refresh the knowledge each term with python run_all.py. The catalog hash check disables stale knowledge automatically.
  6. Agree a CO1 confidence threshold once intake confidence has been calibrated, then set min_confidence.
